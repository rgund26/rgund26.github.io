PHD RESEARCH · PRECISION SENSING

Wireless CO₂
Soil-Respiration Sensor

Custom electronics, a compact protective enclosure and local wireless telemetry for soil-respiration measurements.

Hardware developed · Validation underway

THE CHALLENGE

Bring measurements closer to the soil.

Manual chamber sampling and laboratory analysis can be time-consuming. This project develops a compact CO₂ sensing node for field chambers and laboratory incubation jars, with local access to readings and trends.

MY ENGINEERING WORK

Hardware, firmware and the physical interface.

Electronics

Sensirion SCD-30 and Seeed XIAO ESP32-C3 integrated on a custom PCB, with timestamped measurements and configurable sampling.

Mechanical design

A two-section, threaded PETG capsule separates electronics from the sampling environment. An ePTFE filter protects against moisture while permitting gas exchange.

Data pipeline

Local Wi-Fi nodes feed an ESP32/LoRa relay. A Raspberry Pi 5 hosts logging, trend views, historical filtering and CSV export.

POWER & SAMPLING

A design shaped by deployment.

20 daysReported battery runtime

The project reports this runtime at a one-hour sampling interval with 55 minutes of deep sleep per cycle. Changing the sampling schedule changes the power budget.

The current sensing node measures CO₂, temperature and relative humidity. Support for CH₄ and N₂O is an expansion concept, not a demonstrated measurement capability.

TESTING & VALIDATION

Learning from reference measurements.

Initial testing completed

GC comparisons and gas-response tests have been performed. Recent work examines stabilization time, pressure effects during sampling and passive gas exchange through the enclosure.

These are initial comparisons, not a claim of completed field validation or certified accuracy.

Next validation steps

Further reference comparisons and soil-incubation tests, including soil with glucose and soil-only conditions, are planned to assess response under changing CO₂ concentrations.
