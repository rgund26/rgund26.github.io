PHD RESEARCH · PRECISION SENSING

Wireless CO₂ Soil-Respiration Sensor

I developed a wireless CO₂ sensor for field soil chambers and lab incubation jars.

Two prototypes built · Validation underway

From field chamber to incubation jar

I built the first prototype on perfboard inside a rectangular mesh enclosure. It fit a field chamber. Its size made it impractical for jars, and the open mesh offered limited protection from dust and moisture.

I redesigned it around a custom PCB and a threaded PETG capsule with an ePTFE filter. The compact package fits both lab jars and field chambers.

KiCad, board debugging and low-power design

I learned KiCad to lay out the board for the Sensirion SCD30 and XIAO ESP32-C3. I then assembled the board and debugged the hardware.

The prototype ran for about 20 days with hourly sampling and 55 minutes of deep sleep per cycle.

Device configuration web app

I built a web app that lets users set the sampling interval, connect the device to Wi-Fi, and calibrate the CO₂ sensor wirelessly from a browser. The app also shows live readings and supports CSV export.

Testing and next steps

Initial tests compared CO₂ readings with gas chromatography and checked sensor response time. Further reference comparisons, soil-incubation trials and field evaluation are planned.

PhD research at UT Knoxville. Supported by NSF and presented as an ASABE 2026 poster.
